When to Send an Invoice: A Complete Guide

Updated February 2025 · 5 min read

Timing is everything in invoicing. Send too early and the client may feel rushed. Send too late and you risk being forgotten or deprioritized. The right timing depends on your industry, project type, and client relationship. This guide covers when to send invoices in every common scenario so you can optimize your cash flow and get paid faster.

The Golden Rule: Invoice as Soon as Possible

Research consistently shows that invoices sent promptly get paid faster. According to billing data from thousands of freelancers and small businesses, invoices sent on the day work is completed are paid an average of 14 days sooner than those sent a week later. The reason is simple: the work is fresh in the client's mind, and they feel the value immediately.

Make invoicing part of your project completion checklist. Finish the work, send the deliverables, send the invoice. Same day. No exceptions.

Invoice Timing by Project Type

One-Time Projects

For single, defined projects with a clear start and end date, invoice upon completion and delivery. If the project is large, consider milestone billing (more on that below). For small projects under $1,000, a single invoice upon completion is standard and expected.

When to send: The same day you deliver the final work product.

Retainer or Ongoing Work

For retainer clients or ongoing monthly work, establish a regular billing cycle and stick to it. Most freelancers invoice on the 1st or 15th of each month for the previous period's work. Some invoice at the beginning of the month for the upcoming period (prepaid retainer).

When to send: The same day each month. Set a calendar reminder and never miss it.

Milestone-Based Projects

Large projects benefit from milestone billing, where you invoice at predefined stages. This improves cash flow for you and reduces risk for the client. Common milestone structures include 50/50 (half upfront, half on completion) or thirds (start, midpoint, completion).

When to send: When each milestone is completed and approved by the client.

Hourly Work

If you bill hourly, choose a regular invoicing period — weekly, biweekly, or monthly. Weekly invoicing is best for high-volume hours, while monthly works for low-hour ongoing engagements. Always include a detailed timesheet or hour breakdown with the invoice.

When to send: At the end of each billing period. Weekly billing on Friday or Monday is common.

Deposit or Advance Payment

For new clients or large projects, requesting a deposit before starting work is wise and increasingly standard. Typical deposits range from 25% to 50% of the total project cost. This protects you from non-payment and demonstrates the client's commitment.

When to send: Before starting any work. Don't begin until the deposit is received.

Best Day and Time to Send Invoices

Believe it or not, the day of the week and time of day you send an invoice can affect how quickly it's paid. Here's what the data suggests:

  • Best days: Tuesday through Thursday. Monday inboxes are flooded from the weekend. Friday emails get lost before the weekend.
  • Best time: Morning, between 9-11 AM in the client's time zone. Your invoice appears near the top of their inbox when they start their workday.
  • Avoid: Weekends, holidays, and end-of-day sends. These get buried and forgotten.

That said, don't let timing optimization delay your invoice by days. A promptly sent invoice on a Friday still beats a "perfectly timed" invoice sent the following Tuesday.

Setting Payment Terms

Your payment terms define when the client must pay after receiving the invoice. Common terms include:

  • Due on Receipt: Payment expected immediately. Best for small amounts or new clients.
  • Net 15: Due within 15 days. Good for freelancers who want faster cash flow.
  • Net 30: Due within 30 days. The most common standard in business.
  • Net 60: Due within 60 days. Common with larger corporations. Avoid if possible — it's a long time to wait.
  • 2/10 Net 30: 2% discount if paid within 10 days, otherwise due in 30. Incentivizes early payment.

How to Handle Late Payments

Even with perfect timing, some invoices will go unpaid past the due date. Have a follow-up system in place:

  1. Due date: Send a friendly reminder. "Hi [Name], just a reminder that invoice #INV-042 is due today."
  2. 7 days late: Send a firmer follow-up. "Invoice #INV-042 is now 7 days past due. Please arrange payment at your earliest convenience."
  3. 14 days late: Phone call or direct message in addition to email.
  4. 30+ days late: Send a formal overdue notice with late fee applied (if your terms include one).
  5. 60+ days late: Consider collections, mediation, or discontinuing services.

Special Situations

Client Hasn't Signed the Contract Yet

Never invoice before the contract or agreement is signed. If work has been discussed but not formally agreed upon, sending an invoice is premature and can damage the relationship. Get the agreement in writing first, then invoice according to the agreed terms.

Scope Changed Mid-Project

If the scope increases after the original agreement, document the change in writing (a simple email works) and adjust your invoicing accordingly. You can invoice for the additional work separately or include it in the next scheduled invoice with a clear note about the scope change.

International Clients

When billing international clients, account for currency, time zones, and local payment customs. Some countries have longer standard payment terms. Specify the currency on the invoice and consider international payment methods like Wise or PayPal for easier cross-border transfers.
